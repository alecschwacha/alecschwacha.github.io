Fiducial tracking for feature-poor environments

Localization when the world gives you nothing to lock onto.

Problem
LiDAR-based localization needs geometry to match against. Long runs of uniform pipe, tank walls, and open floor have almost none, and the pose estimate drifts.
Built
A fiducial tracking system that detects placed markers and feeds their observations into the localization stack. This gives the robot absolute references where the environment has no distinct features.

Platform-wide system health

One answer to "can we trust the data right now?"

Problem
An inspection is only worth something if the robot knows where it was and the sensors were actually recording. Failures in either were hard to see while a job was running.
Built
A health framework that connects the whole platform into three categories, Localization, Robot, and System, and reduces each to a go/no-go state. If localization is compromised by a feature-poor scene or a sparse point cloud, it goes no-go, and customer data collection is blocked until it recovers. Engineers use it for diagnostics, and operators see it live in the front end.

Solo project

Nano + Proton Pack

The R&D prototype that took Gecko into piping inspection and became the basis for its next-generation robotic platform.

Problem
Gecko's crawlers were built for large surfaces like boiler walls and tanks. Piping needed a much smaller, self-contained system that could localize itself and tag every ultrasonic reading with a position.
Built
Two parts. Nano is the sensor payload, carrying ultrasonic transducers and a compact LiDAR. The Proton Pack is the mobile compute unit that runs localization and triggers the sensors. Localization is a custom extended Kalman filter fusing LiDAR, IMU, and wheel encoders, running directly on the compute.
